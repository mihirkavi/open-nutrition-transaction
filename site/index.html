The missing layer

Food data tells us what something is.
OpenNutri describes what was acquired.

Nutrition composition is abundant. The structured connection between a real purchase and a person’s chosen nutrition ecosystem is not.

A foundational truth

Purchased does not mean consumed.

OpenNutri preserves this distinction so downstream tools can make honest, transparent decisions instead of turning acquisition into a false health claim.

Mission

Create a common, open language for food and nutrition data.

So people, food businesses, healthcare providers, researchers, public institutions, and technology companies can use nutrition information more effectively—while preserving choice, privacy, provenance, and control.

Vision

Nutrition information should not be trapped.

Not inside labels, menus, receipts, retailer databases, or individual applications. It should be understandable, portable, verifiable, and reusable across the systems that help people eat, shop, research, and stay healthy.

“The producer should not need to know the eventual consumer. The shared language makes future uses possible.”

OpenNutri design principle

Clear boundaries

What OpenNutri is—and is not.

Is OpenNutri a nutrition database?

No. It reuses product identity and composition sources such as GS1, USDA, manufacturer data, restaurant data, and Open Food Facts. A small reference dataset may support examples and research, but the standard does not aim to duplicate the world’s food databases.

Does a purchase prove someone ate the food?

No. OpenNutri standardizes acquisition events and preserves purchase-versus-consumption state. Nutrition applications or people may later confirm or correct consumption outside the merchant event.

Does a retailer expose its customer database?

No. The intended pattern is a narrow, retailer-controlled interface that exports only the authorized nutritional representation of the customer’s own food event.

Is price part of the core standard?

No. A future economics extension can define optional, separately authorized fields for questions such as nutrient density per dollar.

Does OpenNutri make medical decisions?

No. It provides structured, permissioned inputs. Clinical interpretation, diagnosis, treatment, and advice remain with qualified professionals and applications.
